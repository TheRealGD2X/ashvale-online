# The living world (design, from the owner, 2026-09-26)

Goal: it should feel like a real MMO played alone. That means lots of content, and a simulated
population that feels like a server full of people.

## Today
- About 10 simulated players are generated fresh each time you enter a zone, with levels in that zone's band.
- While you're there, they really play: quests, fights, looting, eating, training, grouping and chat.
- Raiders are made at level 60 when you call `/lfm`.
- Only the players in your group are saved.

## The plan
- **A persistent roster.**
  - A few hundred named characters that belong to your save (the "server").
  - Each has a class, level, gear, home zone, personality, play habits, friends and a guild.
- **A clock that only runs while you play.**
  - An hour you play is an hour of progress for everyone. Log off and the world waits for you.
- **There's always someone at your level:**
  - Levelling paces vary per character: racers, steady players and casual players.
  - New players join the server over time and start at level 1.
  - Max-level players roll alts. A few quit, which keeps the numbers steady.
  - Max-level players still visit low zones: farming materials, helping friends, old achievements.
- **The top end moves:**
  - Max-level players gear up, form raid groups and chase server firsts on the same play-time clock.
  - You might race them, or join them.
- **Busy and quiet hours.** The zones near your level are busiest.
- **The same faces:**
  - Regulars recur. They remember grouping with you, and you can add them as friends.
  - Guilds with guild chat.
  - Trade chat and LFG chat, a market whose prices move with what players list and buy, duels in town.
  - World bosses and rare elites that draw a crowd.
- **Cost:**
  - Only players near you are fully simulated.
  - Everyone else progresses as cheap numbers on the play-time clock.
